A good home workspace doesn't need a spare room or an expensive chair from a design catalogue. It needs to do three things well: keep your body comfortable, help you concentrate, and let you switch off when the working day ends.

<!--more-->

Here's how to design one, whatever the size of your home.

## 1. Choose the right spot

Location matters more than furniture. When choosing where to work, consider:

- **Daylight.** A spot near a window improves mood and reduces eye strain. Try to position your desk so the window is to your side rather than directly behind your screen (glare) or behind you (reflections).
- **Separation.** A door you can close is ideal. If that isn't possible, a corner facing away from the main living area, or a bookcase used as a divider, helps.
- **Noise.** Avoid working next to the washing machine or the TV.
- **Consistency.** Working in the same place each day helps your brain associate it with focus.

## 2. Get the ergonomics right

Occupational safety bodies such as the U.S. OSHA and the UK Health and Safety Executive publish free workstation guides. The core principles are consistent:

- **Screen:** top of the screen at or slightly below eye level, roughly an arm's length away.
- **Chair:** feet flat on the floor or a footrest, thighs roughly parallel to the floor, lower back supported.
- **Arms:** elbows close to your body and bent at roughly a right angle, wrists straight while typing.
- **Laptops:** if you use one all day, raise it on a stand and add a separate keyboard and mouse. This is one of the most effective upgrades you can make.

No position is perfect for eight hours. The best posture is your *next* posture. Stand up, stretch and move regularly. Our guide to [essential productivity habits]({{ '/essential-productivity-habits-for-professionals/' | relative_url }}) explains how to build breaks into your day.

## 3. Light it in layers

Combine three types of light:

1. **Natural light** during the day wherever possible.
2. **Task light**, such as an adjustable desk lamp, for paperwork and evenings.
3. **Ambient light** so the room isn't dark around a bright screen, which is tiring for your eyes.

If you take video calls, place your main light source in front of you, not behind. A window or lamp behind you will turn you into a silhouette.

## 4. Manage sound

Soft furnishings such as rugs, curtains, upholstered chairs and full bookshelves absorb echo and make calls sound better. Noise-cancelling headphones help in busy homes. Some people focus better with steady background sound, while others need silence, so experiment.

## 5. Keep the surface clear

Clutter competes for attention. Keep only what you use daily on the desk and give everything else a home: a drawer unit, a shelf or a box that can be put away at the end of the day. Manage cables with simple clips or a tray under the desk. The [minimalist design principles]({{ '/minimalist-interior-design-ideas-for-modern-homes/' | relative_url }}) we use elsewhere in the home work just as well here.

## 6. Make it yours, within reason

A plant, a piece of art or a well-chosen object makes a workspace somewhere you want to be. Keep it to a few items, and favour things that calm rather than distract.

## 7. Build a start and end ritual

Without a commute, work can leak into every hour. Small rituals help:

- **Start:** open the window, make a coffee, review today's priorities.
- **End:** write tomorrow's top three tasks, close the laptop, and if you can, physically put it away or cover the desk.

## Small-space solutions

- **Wall-mounted fold-down desks** disappear when work is done.
- **A dedicated "work box"** holds your laptop, notebook and chargers, so the dining table can return to being a dining table.
- **A cupboard office** (a "cloffice") turns an alcove or wardrobe into a workspace with doors you can close.

## When home isn't working

Even the best home office can feel isolating. Many remote workers mix home days with a few days in a shared space. Read [how coworking spaces are changing modern work]({{ '/how-coworking-spaces-are-changing-modern-work/' | relative_url }}) to see whether that might suit you.
